# Mathematical notation: conventions and ambiguities

This **editorial reference aid** explains recurring differences in meaning across school mathematics, Bachelor’s, Master’s and computer science modules. It supplements the symbol glossary, establishes a **preferred notation for future revisions**, and does not retrospectively alter the 86 original teaching texts. Where subject-specific conventions differ, the **definition in the respective lesson** takes precedence. No automatic search-and-replace operation may erase these distinctions.

## 1. Quantities, indices and exponents {#S1}

  Symbol            Preferred reading                       Context and limitations
  ----------------- --------------------------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  `x`, `y`, `t`     “ex”, “why”, “tee”                      Variable; `t` usually denotes time, but can also be a free parameter in parametric representations
  `xᵢ`, `x_i`       “x sub i”                               Entry in a list or vector; an index is **not an exponent**
  `x²`, `x^2`       “x squared”                             Exponent, not the second component of a vector
  `f(x)`            “f of x”                                Function value; not automatically multiplication `f · x`
  `f′(x)`, `df/dx`  “derivative of f with respect to x”     For multidimensional functions, use the appropriate partial derivative `∂`
  `∂f/∂x`           “partial derivative with respect to x”  All other independent variables are held fixed when taking the partial derivative

## 2. Absolute values, norms, spaces and sets {#S2}

  Symbol            Distinction                               Editorial rule
  ----------------- ----------------------------------------- -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  `|x|`             Absolute value of a number                Specify “absolute value” in the sentence; for sets, `|A|` often denotes the number of elements
  `‖v‖₂`            Euclidean vector norm                     Specify the subscript `₂`; do not confuse it with the absolute value of a number
  `det(A)`, `|A|`   Determinant of a matrix                   Prefer `det(A)` when `|A|` also denotes the cardinality of a set
  `[a,b]`           Closed interval                           In other texts, brackets can also indicate lists of vectors or a commutator
  `ℝⁿ`, `L²`, `H¹`  Very different vector or function spaces  Define the type of space and the indices in the respective lesson

## 3. Sums and matrices {#S3}

  Symbol             Distinction                                      Editorial rule
  ------------------ ------------------------------------------------ -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  `∑`, `Σ`           Capital sigma can denote a sum or name a matrix  Where possible, use `∑` as the **summation symbol**; explicitly write `Σ` and “matrix sigma” for the matrix of singular values
  `Σ` in statistics  Often a covariance matrix                        Explicitly explain it as a covariance matrix; do not read it as a sum
  `Aᵀ`, `V⊤`         Transposed matrix                                Whether real or complex: distinguish the conjugate transpose `A*` where appropriate for complex matrices
  `A⁻¹`              Inverse                                          Defined only for invertible square matrices; do not equate it with the pseudoinverse `A⁺`
  `Av`               Matrix–vector multiplication                     Order matters; matrix products are not generally commutative

## 4. Reused Greek letters {#S4}

  Symbol  Typical meanings                                                       Required contextual clarification
  ------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------- -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  `ε`     Accuracy tolerance, privacy budget                                     Explain the different meanings explicitly in analysis and differential privacy
  `δ`     Delta bound in continuity, error allowance in differential privacy     Do not carry the meaning from one lesson into another
  `λ`     Eigenvalue, multiplier, regularisation weight, process rate            State the dimension, permissible range and meaning in each case
  `μ`     Expected value, measure, Lagrange multiplier, coefficient of friction  Define unambiguously using a concrete example
  `σ`     Standard deviation, singular value, cross-section                      Do not omit the index or unit
  `i`     Imaginary unit or discrete index                                       Explicitly state `i²=−1` for complex exponential functions; distinguish summation indices separately

## 5. Functions, probabilities and units {#S5}

- `P(A)` usually denotes the probability of an event, but in geometry `P` can be the name of a point. Identify the event or point before the formula.
- `F(x)` can be an antiderivative, a cumulative distribution function or a force. It must be interpreted in context.
- `E[X]` is an expected value, not simply a data mean without a probability model.
- `T` can represent time, temperature or matrix transposition. `Aᵀ` is never a temperature without context.
- In `e^(kt)`, the entire product `kt` is the exponent and is **dimensionless**. If time `t` is measured in seconds, `k` typically has the unit `1/s`.
- `log₂` denotes the base-two logarithm; `ln` denotes the natural logarithm. In a real-valued context, the argument of a logarithm must be positive and dimensionless.
- For `∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx`, the variable of integration `x` must be distinguished from the limits; the result and its unit correspond to the **integrand multiplied by the unit of the variable of integration**.
- `p-value`, confidence interval, posterior probability and model confidence are **different concepts**; identical percentages do not make them interchangeable.

## 6. Principles of standardisation {#S6}

1.  **Meaning first, then the formula:** explain an unfamiliar symbol in words before using it, so that it can be read aloud.
2.  **Identify conventions:** state DFT/Fourier sign conventions, matrix dimensions, normalisation, statistical assumptions and boundary conditions.
3.  **No silent normalisation:** retain existing Unicode formulae unchanged; document any subsequent change with the lesson ID, source comparison and a calculation test.
4.  **Use MathML selectively:** represent only manually verified mathematical expressions from `SEMANTISCHE-FORMELN.md` semantically; this is not yet an automatic conversion of the entire course collection.
5.  **Take validity conditions seriously:** real versus complex numbers, domains, regularity and discrete versus continuous models must not be obscured by a uniform visual notation.

## Master’s-level and AI notation {#S7}

- **Stochastic row vectors:** In M3-L4, `πP = π` describes a stationary row distribution; for column vectors, the usual expression is instead `Pᵀπ = π`. The convention must not change unnoticed.
- **Time and iteration indices:** In `yₙ₊₁` of Euler’s method and `xₜ` of the diffusion model, the subscripts indicate steps, not powers.
- **Operators with the same name:** `P` denotes a projection in M3-L2, a transition matrix in M3-L4, a Riccati coefficient in M3-L12 and probability notation in M4-L2. The respective context determines the meaning.
- **Tilde, hat and bar:** `D̃` is a modified degree matrix, `p̂` an estimated parameter and `āₜ` a product of several signal factors. Different diacritical marks are not interchangeable.
- **`mod`, `do` and `max`:** These are operation names, not letters multiplied together. They are therefore typeset upright in MathML formulae.
- **Squared norm and indices:** `‖v‖₂²` denotes the square of the Euclidean norm; the subscript two selects the norm, and the superscript two is the power.

## Sources, presentation and prerequisites {#S8}

- The **original expression in the lesson** takes precedence; MathML represents an individually verified variant of the notation and must not silently reinterpret the formula.
- In M2-L4, `f⁻¹(U)` denotes the **preimage** of a set, not necessarily the inverse function; a non-injective function can still have preimages.
- `|v|` and `‖v‖` denote Euclidean lengths in the current context; the norm must always be named before it is used.
- The epsilon–delta formula in M1-L3 quantifies over **all admissible x in the domain**. The distinction between a selected sample and a general proof must be explicitly recognised.
- For `P(A|B)`, `P(B)>0` is a prerequisite. In Newton’s iteration, the derivative at the current step must be non-zero; this alone does not guarantee convergence.

## Semantic reading of long expressions {#S9}

- In the HTML output, a semantically structured MathML formula is preceded by the **verbatim, verified original notation**.
- Two linked paragraphs explain the expression in terms of its **spoken reading** and its **validity conditions**. The reading does not replace the full context in the teaching text.
- For a smooth interior extremum, an expression such as `f′(x₀)=0` is a **necessary**, not a sufficient condition.
- The infinite geometric series `1+q+q²+…=1/(1−q)` holds for `|q|<1`, not merely for an unspecified “small q”.
- In optimisation, dimensional information, the domain and boundary or transversality conditions must be checked separately; Unicode blocks are not translated automatically.
