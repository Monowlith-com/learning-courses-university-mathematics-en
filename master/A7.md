# Compulsory electives and modern specialisations

## What do compulsory electives mean in a degree programme? {#S1}

**Compulsory module:** Required in the respective degree programme. **Compulsory elective module:** Students must select modules from a prescribed range in accordance with the programme regulations. **Elective module/free elective area:** An opportunity to shape one's studies, the scope and credit eligibility of which depend on the degree programme. **Seminar/project:** A form of teaching, not automatically a separate qualification level. **Track/profile:** A set of related modules, depending on the university. These terms are not defined identically everywhere; the respective study and examination regulations remain authoritative.

## Why is there an elective module M4? {#S2}

School mathematics (40 lessons) and university modules M1–M3 (34 lessons) are available as reading materials. Modern computer science and AI degree programmes also include specialised compulsory elective areas. M4 is therefore **not a new compulsory semester**, but a flexible collection of reading materials with three profiles: AI models, dependable computer science, and robotics/networks.

The twelve lessons listed here, M4-L1–M4-L12, are optional specialisations. They do not correspond to a prescribed set of study regulations or an official ECTS allocation.

## Optional subject areas and learning paths {#S4}

The twelve elective topics presented here in M4 are: mathematical foundations of language models, causality, graph networks, formal software correctness, cryptography, reinforcement learning, differential privacy, generative models, AI reliability, distributed systems, sensor fusion and vector search.

Example learning paths: **privacy protection** M1-L7 → M3-L5 → M4-L5 → M4-L7; **generative AI** M2-L11 → M3-L6 → M4-L1 → M4-L8; **robust AI** M1-L7 → M3-L5 → M2-L11 → M4-L9. These paths are educational suggestions, not study regulations. The precise choice of topics depends on interests and prior knowledge.
