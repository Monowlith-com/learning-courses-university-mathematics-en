## [M1-L10](index.html?course=school_mathematics&module=M1&lesson=L10&syslang=en&doclang=de&theme=light) – Pythagoras' theorem (school lesson in German) {#S1}

**Original notation:** `a² + b² = c²`.

**Spoken:** a squared plus b squared equals c squared.

**Prerequisites:** Only for a right-angled triangle; c is the hypotenuse.

$$
a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}
$$

## [M1-L13](index.html?course=school_mathematics&module=M1&lesson=L13&syslang=en&doclang=de&theme=light) – Quadratic formula (school lesson in German) {#S2}

**Original notation:** `x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac))/(2a)`.

**Spoken:** x equals minus b plus or minus the square root of b squared minus four a c, all divided by two a.

**Prerequisites:** For $ax^2+bx+c=0$ with a non-zero; real solutions exist only for a non-negative discriminant.

$$
x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}
$$

## [M2-L9](index.html?course=school_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L9&syslang=en&doclang=de&theme=light) – Fundamental theorem of calculus (school lesson in German) {#S3}

**Original notation:** `∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a)`.

**Spoken:** The integral from a to b of f of x with respect to x equals F of b minus F of a.

**Prerequisites:** f is continuous on the interval; F is an antiderivative of f.

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=F(b)-F(a)
$$

## [M2-L16](index.html?course=school_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L16&syslang=en&doclang=de&theme=light) – Expected value of a discrete random variable (school lesson in German) {#S4}

**Original notation:** `E[X] = Σᵢ xᵢ P(X = xᵢ)`.

**Spoken:** The expected value is the sum of all values multiplied by their respective probabilities.

**Prerequisites:** For finitely many values, or for infinitely many values provided that the weighted values are absolutely summable.

$$
\mathrm{E}[X]=\sum_i x_i\,\mathrm{P}(X=x_i)
$$

## [M1-L4](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M1&lesson=L4&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Gradient in two variables {#S5}

**Original notation:** `∇f = (∂f/∂x, ∂f/∂y)`.

**Spoken:** Nabla f is a vector consisting of the two partial derivatives.

**Prerequisites:** f must possess the indicated partial derivatives at the point under consideration.

$$
\nabla f=\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x},\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)
$$

## [M1-L6](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M1&lesson=L6&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Eigenvalue equation {#S6}

**Original notation:** `A v = λ v`.

**Spoken:** Matrix A multiplied by vector v equals lambda multiplied by vector v.

**Prerequisites:** v must not be the zero vector; lambda is the corresponding eigenvalue.

$$
Av=\lambda v
$$

## [M2-L9](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L9&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Discrete Fourier transform {#S7}

**Original notation:** `X[k] = Σₙ₌₀ᴺ⁻¹ x[n] e^(−2πikn/N)`.

**Spoken:** The frequency value at index k is the sum of the sampled values multiplied by a complex oscillation.

**Prerequisites:** N is a positive integer; i is the imaginary unit; the sign and normalisation conventions are explicitly chosen.

$$
X[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-\frac{2\pi i kn}{N}}
$$

## [M2-L10](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L10&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Shannon entropy {#S8}

**Original notation:** `H(X) = −Σᵢ pᵢ log₂(pᵢ)`.

**Spoken:** Entropy is minus the sum of each probability multiplied by the base-two logarithm of that probability.

**Prerequisites:** The p values form a probability distribution; for $p=0$, the summand is set to 0 by its limit.

$$
H(X)=-\sum_i p_i\log_2(p_i)
$$

## [M3-L6](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L6&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – KKT complementarity {#S9}

**Original notation:** `λᵢ gᵢ(x*) = 0`.

**Spoken:** Lambda subscript i multiplied by the i-th constraint function at the optimum equals zero.

**Prerequisites:** For inequality constraints $g_i(x)\le0$ and $\lambda_i\ge0$; further conditions apply to full KKT optimality statements.

$$
\lambda_i g_i(x^{\ast})=0
$$

## [M3-L7](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L7&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Singular value decomposition {#S10}

**Original notation:** `A = U Σ Vᵀ`.

**Spoken:** A equals U times sigma times V transpose.

**Prerequisites:** For real matrices with compatible dimensions; sigma contains non-negative singular values.

A=U\Sigma V^{\mathrm{T}}

## [M3-L10](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L10&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Fourier transform with angular frequency {#S11}

**Original notation:** `f̂(ξ) = ∫₋∞^∞ f(x) e^(−iξx) dx`.

**Spoken:** f hat of xi is the integral from minus infinity to plus infinity of the function multiplied by a complex oscillation.

## [M4-L1](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L1&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Scaled dot-product attention {#S12}

**Original notation:** `Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(Q Kᵀ / √dₖ) V`.

**Spoken:** Attention is the softmax of the scaled product matrix of queries and keys, multiplied by the value vectors.

**Prerequisites:** The matrix dimensions must be compatible; exclude masked positions from the softmax where applicable.

$$
\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^{\mathrm{T}}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V

## [M4-L7](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L7&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Differential privacy guarantee {#S13}

**Original notation:** `Pr[M(D)∈S] ≤ e^ε Pr[M(D′)∈S] + δ`.

**Spoken:** The probability of an output set S for neighbouring datasets is at most e to the power of epsilon times the other probability plus delta.

**Prerequisites:** D and D′ are neighbouring datasets; the statement applies to every admissible output set S.

$$
\Pr[M(D)\in S]\le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[M(D')\in S]+\delta
$$

## [M4-L9](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L9&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Brier score for binary prediction {#S14}

**Original notation:** `BS = (1/n) Σᵢ (pᵢ − yᵢ)²`.

**Spoken:** The Brier score is the mean squared difference between the predicted probability and the binary outcome.

**Prerequisites:** Here the binary variant with n observations; $p_i\in[0,1]$ and $y_i\in\{0,1\}$.

$$
\mathrm{BS}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i{(p_i-y_i)}^2
$$

## [M4-L11](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L11&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Scalar Kalman gain {#S15}

**Original notation:** `K = P⁻/(P⁻ + R)`.

**Spoken:** The Kalman gain is the prediction variance divided by the sum of the prediction variance and the measurement error variance.

**Prerequisites:** A scalar linear model with appropriately modelled, uncorrelated errors and positive total variance.

$$
K=\frac{P^{-}}{P^{-}+R}
$$

## [M4-L12](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L12&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Cosine similarity {#S16}

**Original notation:** `sim_cos(q,x) = (q·x)/(‖q‖₂ ‖x‖₂)`.

**Spoken:** Cosine similarity is the dot product divided by the product of the two vector lengths.

**Prerequisites:** Both vectors must be non-zero.

$$
\operatorname{sim}_{\cos}(q,x)=\frac{q\cdot x}{\lVert q\rVert_2\lVert x\rVert_2}
$$

## [M2-L15](index.html?course=school_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L15&syslang=en&doclang=de&theme=light) – Binomial probability (school lesson in German) {#S18}

**Original notation:** `P(X=k) = C(n,k) · p^k · (1−p)^(n−k)`.

**Spoken:** The probability of exactly k successes is the binomial coefficient n choose k, times p to the power k, times one minus p to the power n minus k.

**Prerequisites:** n independent Bernoulli trials with the same success probability $0\le p\le1$; n is a natural number and $0\le k\le n$. $C(n,k)$ counts the possible selections of successes.

$$
P(X=k)=C(n,k)\cdot p^k\cdot{(1-p)}^{n-k}
$$

## [M1-L9](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M1&lesson=L9&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Explicit Euler method {#S19}

**Original notation:** `yₙ₊₁ = yₙ + h · f(tₙ, yₙ)`.

**Spoken:** The y value at the next step equals the y value at the current step plus step size h times the rate-of-change function at the current time and state.

**Prerequisites:** Given the initial value $y_0$ and differential equation $y'=f(t,y)$; $h>0$. A numerical approximation, not a general exact solution or a stability guarantee.

$$
y_{n+1}=y_n+h\cdot f(t_n,y_n)
$$

## [M2-L8](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L8&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – One-dimensional heat equation {#S20}

**Original notation:** `∂u/∂t = α · ∂²u/∂x²`.

**Spoken:** The partial derivative of temperature with respect to time equals alpha times the second partial derivative with respect to position.

**Prerequisites:** An idealised one-dimensional diffusion model with constant positive diffusion coefficient $\alpha$; initial and boundary conditions are part of the specific solution.

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\alpha\cdot\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}
$$

## [M2-L12](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L12&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Forward kinematics of a planar two-joint arm {#S21}

**Original notation:** `x = l₁ cos(q₁) + l₂ cos(q₁+q₂)`.

**Spoken:** The horizontal tool coordinate is the first arm length times the cosine of the first joint angle plus the second arm length times the cosine of the sum of the angles.

**Prerequisites:** Rigid planar links, rotation angles in radians and a specified coordinate origin; joint play and elasticity are not modelled.

$$
x=l_1\cos(q_1)+l_2\cos(q_1+q_2)
$$

## [M3-L1](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L1&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Dominated convergence theorem {#S22}

**Original notation:** `lim(n→∞) ∫_Ω fₙ dμ = ∫_Ω f dμ`.

**Spoken:** The limit of the integrals of the sequence of functions equals the integral of their limiting function.

**Prerequisites:** $f_n$ is measurable, $f_n\to f$ almost everywhere, and $|f_n|\le g$ almost everywhere for a single integrable dominating function g. Without these conditions the equality can fail.

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_\Omega f_n\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_\Omega f\,\mathrm{d}\mu
$$

## [M3-L2](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L2&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Idempotence of a projection operator {#S23}

**Original notation:** `P² = P`.

**Spoken:** Applying a projection operator twice gives the same result as applying it once.

**Prerequisites:** The equality holds for a projection operator such as the mean-value projection defined in M3-L2. $P^2=P$ alone does not establish orthogonality.

$$
P^2=P
$$

## [M3-L3](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L3&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Weak form of the one-dimensional Poisson equation {#S24}

**Original notation:** `∫₀¹ u′(x) v′(x) dx = ∫₀¹ v(x) dx`.

**Spoken:** The integral of the derivative of the unknown function times the derivative of the test function equals the integral of the test function.

**Prerequisites:** $-u''=1$ on (0,1) with $u(0)=u(1)=0$; test functions v with vanishing boundary values. In the Sobolev formulation, $u,v\in H_0^1(0,1)$.

$$
\int_0^1 u'(x)v'(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_0^1 v(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
$$

## [M3-L4](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L4&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Stationary distribution of a Markov chain {#S25}

**Original notation:** `πP = π`.

**Spoken:** A row vector pi remains unchanged after multiplication by the transition matrix P.

**Prerequisites:** P is a row-stochastic transition matrix; $\pi$ is a non-negative row distribution whose entries sum to one. Existence and uniqueness must be checked separately.

$$
\pi P=\pi
$$

## [M3-L5](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L5&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Maximum likelihood estimation in the Bernoulli model {#S26}

**Original notation:** `p̂ = k/n`.

**Spoken:** p hat is the number of observed successes divided by the number of trials.

**Prerequisites:** $n\ge1$ independent and identically Bernoulli-distributed observations; k is the number of ones. This is an MLE in the specific Bernoulli model, not a general estimation formula.

$$
\hat p=\frac{k}{n}
$$

## [M3-L8](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L8&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Riemannian metric on the sphere {#S27}

**Original notation:** `ds² = R² dθ² + R² sin²(θ) dφ²`.

**Spoken:** The square of the infinitesimal line element equals the radius squared times the change in angle theta squared plus the radius squared times the sine squared of theta times the change in angle phi squared.

**Prerequisites:** A sphere of fixed radius $R>0$; $\theta$ is the polar angle, $\varphi$ is longitude; spherical coordinates are not regular at the poles.

$$
{(\mathrm{d}s)}^2=R^2{(\mathrm{d}\theta)}^2+R^2\sin^2(\theta){(\mathrm{d}\varphi)}^2
$$

## [M3-L9](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L9&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Orthogonality of a planar rotation matrix {#S28}

**Original notation:** `R(θ)ᵀR(θ) = I`.

**Spoken:** The transpose of the rotation matrix multiplied by the original rotation matrix gives the identity matrix.

**Prerequisites:** $R(\theta)$ is the real rotation of the plane through angle $\theta$ specified in M3-L9; length preservation follows. A proper rotation also satisfies $\det R=1$.

$$
{R(\theta)}^{\mathrm{T}}R(\theta)=I
$$

## [M3-L11](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L11&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Quadratic Tikhonov regularisation {#S29}

**Original notation:** `Jλ(x) = ‖Ax−b‖₂² + λ‖x‖₂²`.

**Spoken:** The objective J subscript lambda of x is the squared data error plus lambda times the squared vector norm of the unknown parameters.

**Prerequisites:** Here the Euclidean norm and $\lambda>0$ are used; matrix A and observation b must have compatible dimensions. Regularisation affects bias and stability.

$$
J_\lambda(x)=\lVert Ax-b\rVert_2^2+\lambda\lVert x\rVert_2^2
$$

## [M3-L12](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M3&lesson=L12&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Positive Riccati solution in the scalar LQR example {#S30}

**Original notation:** `P = √2 − 1`.

**Spoken:** P equals the square root of two minus one.

**Prerequisites:** For the unconstrained continuous system $\dot x=-x+u$ specified in M3-L12 with cost $\int_0^\infty(x^2+u^2)\,\mathrm{d}t$; P is the positive stabilising solution of the algebraic Riccati equation $P^2+2P-1=0$.

$$
P=\sqrt 2-1
$$

## [M4-L2](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L2&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Backdoor adjustment for a causal intervention {#S31}

**Original notation:** `P(Y=y | do(X=x)) = Σ_z P(Y=y | X=x,Z=z) · P(Z=z)`.

**Spoken:** The distribution after an intervention is summed over the background groups Z: the observed conditional outcome distribution times the frequency of the respective group.

**Prerequisites:** Z must be an admissible backdoor adjustment set relative to the causal graph; no intervention on Z, no remaining unobserved confounding on the relevant paths, and the required positivity.

$$
P(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))=\sum_z P(Y=y\mid X=x,Z=z)\cdot P(Z=z)
$$

## [M4-L3](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L3&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Symmetrically normalised graph convolution {#S32}

**Original notation:** `H_neu = σ(D̃^(−1/2) Ã D̃^(−1/2) H_alt W)`.

**Spoken:** The new node features result from a non-linear activation of the neighbourhood product, degree-normalised on both sides, with the previous features and weights.

**Prerequisites:** An undirected graph with self-loops, hence a positive degree matrix after adding the self-loops; compatible matrix dimensions and componentwise activation.

$$
H_{\mathrm{neu}}=\sigma\left(\tilde D^{-\frac12}\tilde A\tilde D^{-\frac12}H_{\mathrm{alt}}W\right)
$$

## [M4-L4](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L4&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Hoare triple for an assignment {#S33}

**Original notation:** `{x≥0} x:=x+1 {x≥1}`.

**Spoken:** If x is at least zero before the instruction, x is at least one after incrementing it by one.

**Prerequisites:** A classical integer or real-valued state variable without overflow; the instruction must terminate and the assignment operator must have the stated semantics.

$$
\{x\geq0\}\ x\coloneqq x+1\ \{x\geq1\}
$$

## [M4-L5](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L5&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – RSA encryption as a modular power expression {#S34}

**Original notation:** `c = m^e mod n`.

**Spoken:** The ciphertext is the message raised to the public exponent, after which the remainder upon division by n is taken.

**Prerequisites:** This is only the basic mathematical RSA operation, not a secure full implementation; real schemes require validated padding, key sizes and secure libraries.

$$
c=m^e\bmod n
$$

## [M4-L6](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L6&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Bellman optimality equation {#S35}

**Original notation:** `V*(s) = max_a Σ_s′ P(s′|s,a) [R(s,a,s′)+γV*(s′)]`.

**Spoken:** The optimal state value is the maximum over actions of the expected immediate reward plus the discounted optimal successor value.

**Prerequisites:** A finite, fully observed, discounted Markov decision process with $0\le\gamma<1$ and bounded rewards. The equation gives no guarantee of model fidelity in real environments.

$$
V^{\ast}(s)=\max_a\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\left[R(s,a,s')+\gamma V^{\ast}(s')\right]
$$

## [M4-L8](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L8&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Forward noising in a diffusion model {#S36}

**Original notation:** `xₜ = √āₜ · x₀ + √(1−āₜ) · ε`.

**Spoken:** The noisy vector at step t consists of an attenuated original vector plus a normally distributed noise vector with the remaining variance share.

**Prerequisites:** $\bar\alpha_t$ is the product of the signal shares of the steps; $\varepsilon$ is independently standard normally distributed. The formula describes the forward process conditional on the original $x_0$.

$$
x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\cdot x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\cdot\varepsilon
$$

## [M4-L10](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M4&lesson=L10&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Value of a replicated G-counter {#S37}

**Original notation:** `value(s) = Σᵢ sᵢ`.

**Spoken:** The counter value read out is the sum of all componentwise counter values.

**Prerequisites:** A grow-only counter CRDT in which each replica monotonically increases its own component; merging takes the componentwise maximum, not the sum of the complete replica values.

$$
\operatorname{value}(s)=\sum_i s_i
$$

**Prerequisites:** Both functions are differentiable at the point under consideration.

$$
{(f\cdot g)}'(x)=f'(x)\cdot g(x)+f(x)\cdot g'(x)
$$

## [M2-L8](index.html?course=school_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L8&syslang=en&doclang=de&theme=light) – Signed definite integral (school lesson in German) {#S47}

**Original notation:** `∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a)`.

**Spoken:** The integral from a to b of f of x with respect to x equals the antiderivative F at b minus the antiderivative F at a.

**Prerequisites:** f is continuous on the closed interval between a and b; F is an antiderivative there. The integral is an oriented net total.

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=F(b)-F(a)
$$

## [M2-L11](index.html?course=school_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L11&syslang=en&doclang=de&theme=light) – Dot product from coordinates (school lesson in German) {#S48}

**Original notation:** `u⃗ · v⃗ = u₁v₁ + u₂v₂ + u₃v₃`.

**Spoken:** Vector u dotted with vector v equals the sum of the products of their corresponding components.

**Prerequisites:** Both vectors are three-dimensional and represented in the same orthonormal basis.

$$
\vec u\cdot\vec v=u_1v_1+u_2v_2+u_3v_3
$$

## [M2-L13](index.html?course=school_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L13&syslang=en&doclang=de&theme=light) – Distance from a point to a plane (school lesson in German) {#S49}

**Original notation:** `d(Q,E) = |n⃗ · (q⃗ − p⃗)| / ‖n⃗‖`.

**Spoken:** The distance from point Q to plane E is the absolute value of the dot product of the normal vector and the difference vector, divided by the norm of the normal vector.

**Prerequisites:** The plane contains point p; n is a non-zero normal vector and all quantities are Euclidean.

$$
d(Q,E)=\frac{\left|\vec n\cdot(\vec q-\vec p)\right|}{\lVert\vec n\rVert}
$$

## [M1-L1](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M1&lesson=L1&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Universal quantifier and squares {#S50}

**Original notation:** `∀ x ∈ ℝ : x² ≥ 0`.

**Spoken:** For every real number x, x squared is greater than or equal to zero.

**Prerequisites:** Real numbers with their usual order and multiplication; for complex numbers the order relation is not defined in this way.

$$
\forall x\in\mathbb R:\ x^2\geq0
$$

## [M1-L3](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M1&lesson=L3&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Epsilon–delta condition for continuity {#S51}

**Original notation:** (two lines in the lesson text) `∀ ε > 0 ∃ δ > 0 :` followed on the next line by `|x−x₀| < δ ⇒ |f(x)−f(x₀)| < ε`.

**Spoken:** For every positive epsilon there is a positive delta such that whenever the distance from x to x nought is less than delta, the distance between the function values is less than epsilon.

**Prerequisites:** The comparison holds for all x in the domain of the function; one-sided boundary points are treated relative to the domain accordingly.

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0:\ |x-x_0|<\delta\Rightarrow|f(x)-f(x_0)|<\varepsilon
$$

## [M1-L5](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M1&lesson=L5&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Additivity of a linear map {#S52}

**Original notation:** `T(u⃗+v⃗) = T(u⃗)+T(v⃗)`.

**Spoken:** T of vector u plus vector v equals T of vector u plus T of vector v.

**Prerequisites:** u and v belong to a vector space; T is linear. The scaling property is additionally required.

$$
T(\vec u+\vec v)=T(\vec u)+T(\vec v)
$$

## [M1-L7](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M1&lesson=L7&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Conditional probability {#S53}

**Original notation:** `P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)`.

**Spoken:** The probability of A given B is the probability of their intersection divided by the probability of B.

**Prerequisites:** A and B are events in the same probability space; P of B is positive.

$$
P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}
$$

## [M1-L8](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M1&lesson=L8&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Newton iteration {#S54}

**Original notation:** `xₖ₊₁ = xₖ − f(xₖ) / f′(xₖ)`.

**Spoken:** The next approximation equals the current approximation minus the function value divided by the derivative at the current approximation.

**Prerequisites:** The denominator is non-zero; convergence requires additional local conditions.

$$
x_{k+1}=x_k-\frac{f(x_k)}{f'(x_k)}
$$

## [M2-L1](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L1&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Binomial coefficient using factorials {#S55}

**Original notation:** `C(n,k) = n! / (k! · (n−k)!)`.

**Spoken:** n choose k equals n factorial divided by the product of k factorial and n minus k factorial.

**Prerequisites:** Integers with n greater than or equal to zero and k between zero and n.

$$
C(n,k)=\frac{n!}{k!\cdot(n-k)!}
$$

## [M2-L2](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L2&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – One-dimensional gradient step {#S56}

**Original notation:** `xₙ₊₁ = xₙ − α · f′(xₙ)`.

**Spoken:** The new value is the old value minus learning rate alpha times the derivative at the old point.

**Prerequisites:** A differentiable objective function; alpha greater than zero. A decrease is not guaranteed without a suitable choice of step size.

$$
x_{n+1}=x_n-\alpha\cdot f'(x_n)
$$

## [M2-L4](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L4&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – Preimage of an interval under the square function {#S57}

**Original notation:** `f⁻¹((1,4)) = (−2,−1) ∪ (1,2)`.

**Spoken:** Under f of x equals x squared, the preimage of the open interval one to four consists of the two open intervals minus two to minus one and one to two.

**Prerequisites:** Here f is the square function on the real numbers; the interval endpoints do not belong to the preimage.

$$
f^{-1}((1,4))=(-2,-1)\cup(1,2)
$$

## [M2-L7](index.html?course=university_mathematics&module=M2&lesson=L7&syslang=en&doclang=en&theme=light) – L-two norm of a real function {#S58}

**Original notation:** `‖f‖₂ = √( ∫ von a bis b |f(x)|² dx )`.

**Spoken:** The L-two norm of f is the square root of the integral of the squared absolute value of the function over the interval a to b.

**Prerequisites:** f is square-integrable on the interval; ordinary length measure is used here.

$$
\lVert f\rVert_2=\sqrt{\int_a^b|f(x)|^2\,\mathrm{d}x}
$$
